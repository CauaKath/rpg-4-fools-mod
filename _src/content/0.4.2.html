What the wall keeps

A plant that died on a wall used to leave almost nothing behind. Most of it was swept away by the wall putting itself back in order, and the little that survived was the same flat sprite in every cell. Now the whole plant stays, shaped the way it grew.

One

The wall stopped sweeping itself clean

Only the cell a vine was sown into stands on its own. Every other cell is held up by that root, and turns back into bare panel the moment it cannot find one — which is what lets you break a plant off a wall and keep the wall.

A season used to kill the plant one cell at a time, and the root was as likely to go first as last. When it did, the reversion swept outward and wiped the rest of the plant clean before the season had reached it. Which cells kept their remains came down to the order they happened to be visited in, so a wall that had been covered in vine came out mostly bare with a few patches left on it.

The season now takes the whole plant down in one go, root last. By the time the root is gone there is nothing left for the wall to tidy away. Breaking a living plant still leaves clean panels — that has always been the right answer for a plant you took yourself, and it is untouched.

Two

Debris shaped like the plant that made it

A vine spreads through a box three cells wide and three tall, and every cell knows where it sits in that box. The panel underneath did not, so growth that died on it had nothing to draw from and fell back on one sprite repeated across the whole plant. A trellis never had this problem: an empty stick remembers whether it was the foot, the middle or the crown, and its remains are drawn to match.

The panel now keeps that address too. Six sheets cover the box — three down the middle, three for the arms, mirrored on the left the way the living plant already is.

Centre · bottom

Sits in the farmland. Reaches up and out to both arms, never down.

Centre · middle

The only cell that joins on all four sides, so it carries the most.

Centre · top

The crown of the plant. Hangs down, with nothing above it.

Arm

Reaches back toward the root and never outward, since the box ends there.

The sheets are built out of the trellis remains rather than drawn to sit beside them: the same tangle, turned on its side as well as upright for the middle column, so the four colours match by construction instead of by eye.
Three

Growth that carries across the cells

A trellis stacks without a visible break because the bottom two rows of a stick are the same pixels as its top two, so one section's remains run straight into the next. A wall needs that across as well as up. The wall sheets take the trellis' own joining rows for the vertical meeting and the same rows turned sideways for the horizontal one, so every join in the box is exact rather than close.

Four times the growth

The old sheet drew barely a tenth of its pixels. It was the thinnest thing in the mod's dead art by a wide margin, which is most of why a dead wall looked nothing like a dead trellis.

The wall is still yours

A dead vine is debris on a panel and nothing more. The wall it grew on is untouched, and sowing the cell again clears the remains.

Existing walls are fine

Anything already standing keeps standing. Plants growing now go on growing, and the new shape only shows on the next one to die.

Nothing else moved

Seasons, crops, trellises and compost are untouched. This release is one plant learning to die properly.
