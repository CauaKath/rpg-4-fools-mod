0.4.1 · Released 1 Sep 2026

A quieter turn of the day

A fixes release. The world stopped redrawing itself every morning, the season tint is worked out once a day instead of once per block, and the date is sent when it changes rather than twenty times a second. Nothing new to plant.

One

Dawn no longer blanks the world

The season tint is not painted over the world at render time. It is baked into the chunks themselves when they are built, which is what lets a hillside of grass carry the colour of the month at no cost per frame. The catch is that the tint only reaches the world when the chunks are rebuilt, and the tint moves every in-game day.

That daily rebuild was being asked for in the bluntest way available: throw the whole renderer away and start again. Doing so frees every chunk the graphics card is holding, so for the second or two it takes to build them back, there is nothing to draw. The world went empty and filled back in, once every morning, in full view.

It now asks for a rebuild instead of a rebuild from nothing. The chunks already on the graphics card keep being drawn while the new ones are prepared behind them, and each is swapped in as it finishes. The same work happens; you no longer watch it happen.

The colours still turn

The cached biome colours are cleared first, so a rebuilt chunk asks what month it is rather than reusing yesterday's answer.

Once a day, as before

The rebuild happens on the same schedule it always did. This changes how it is asked for, not how often.

Two

The colour of the month, worked out once

Every tinted block asks what colour it should be: grass, leaves, vines, ferns, sugar cane, lily pads. The answer is the current sub-season's grade eased towards the next one, according to how far through the month you are — three numbers, a hue shift and two multipliers.

Those three numbers were being worked out from scratch for every single block face, on every rebuild, to arrive at the same answer every time. They depend on nothing but the date, and the date moves once a day.

They are now worked out once and kept until the date moves. A block face compares two values and reads the answer. The fog and sky did the same arithmetic every frame, and now do the same comparison.

Three

The date is sent when it changes

The server told every player the date twenty times a second, for a date that changes once every twenty minutes. The client threw away all but the first of them — it checks whether the day has actually moved before doing anything — so this was twenty-four thousand messages a day to deliver one fact.

It is now sent when the day turns, and to a player as they join, since they have missed the last one. Everything that hung off it went the same way: a persistent-state lookup, an allocation and a write to every player's saved data, all of which happened every tick and now happen once a day.

Before After
Date messages 24,000 1

Also

Two things that were hiding behind it

Sending the date constantly was covering for two bugs. Neither could be seen while every tick papered over the last one, and both had to be fixed before the sending could stop.

The HUD survives a respawn

The date was kept on your player, and your player is rebuilt when you die or change dimension — so the date was lost each time, and silently refilled a tick later. It is no longer kept somewhere that gets thrown away.

A second world is its own world

The handler that watches the calendar outlives the world in single player. A second world inherited the first one's season and ran a season change across every loaded chunk on its first tick. It now starts fresh.

The banner reads the clock

The day-change message fades in and out over its first eight seconds. It takes its timing from the world clock now, which the client keeps for itself.

Nothing else moved

No crops, sprites, recipes or seasons changed. Existing worlds carry on exactly as they were.
