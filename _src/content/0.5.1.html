Vine, timber and soil

Four things you had been looking at without them being right. A cucumber with a stick through it, compost soil that read as a different block dropped into a field, dead growth that kept none of the plant's shape, and a trellis that was a ladder. No behaviour changed: every one of these is what the same block already did, drawn properly.

One

The cucumber had a stick through it

A vine spreading over a wall has to meet itself on all four sides, and the sheets were built to guarantee that: a stem cross at the same rows and the same columns in every cell, so any two cells joined in any direction without either one knowing which neighbours it had. It worked, and it was drawn in the plain cucumber's own brown, which meant every covered cell had timber running through the middle of it. At the young ages it was worse — a bare brown pole standing clear of a few leaves.

The cross is gone at every age. It cost almost nothing to lose, because it sat at exactly the rows and columns the panel's own post and rungs occupy: the young cells come out pixel for pixel what they were, since the pole was behind the post the whole time and was only ever visible on the sheet. What changed in world is the horizontal runner, the one part that ran out past the post. Cells meet through foliage now, which the doubled, shifted foliage is dense enough to do.

Two

Compost soil that is soil

Farmland worked with compost had its own art: its own four browns, its own clumped pattern, drawn from scratch. Minecraft's farmland is not clumped noise — it is two furrow troughs running down the texture — and none of that survived being redrawn, so a compost plot read as a different block dropped into a field. The sides never had the problem, because they were always plain dirt.

The tops are Minecraft's own farmland now, and the only thing drawn on them is the catalyst: six flecks of two pixels each, twelve pixels of two hundred and fifty six. A fleck is worked out from the soil pixel it lands on rather than named outright, pulled towards the catalyst's colour and then pushed off the soil's own brightness, which is what lets one rule serve a furrow and a ridge, and dry ground and wet.

Rich · humus and straw

Dark clods with the straw run through them. Wet, the humus has nothing to read against — soaked farmland is already as dark — so what shows is the straw.

Warm · ash

Pale grey grit, the brightest thing on the tile, which is what ash is. Kept neutral so a wet rich plot is never mistaken for it.

Creeping · bark and moss

Tan chips with a duller green between them — the only colour in the set that is not soil, which is what lets it carry on so few pixels.

Watered

Each kind has a wet sheet, and it falls out of the same rule rather than being drawn again: the flecks go down with the soil.

Three

Dead growth shaped like what died

Keeping the plant's shape was the whole point of the wall's dead sheets, and they did not. Two fifths of their pixels sat where the living plant had never grown, spread evenly over the tile, so a dead wall read as straw hanging in the air rather than as a vine that died where it hung.

Each dead cell is now derived from the living cell of the same arm and row, and nothing is added to it. The only thing taken away is the brightest quarter — the lit faces of the leaves — because leaf dries and falls while stem stays. What is left is the plant's own structure rather than a sample of it, and it regenerates from the living art, so it cannot drift out of date again the way the last set did.

Four

The trellis is a lattice

Outside the post, a panel was four full-width rails and a dozen bare rows: a ladder, with nothing running the other way for a tendril to catch. It has uprights now, drawn behind the rails so a crossing reads as rail in front of stile, and the two arms differ properly — where their uprights sit, which rail is the thick one, where the nail goes. They used to differ by one nail each, two pixels out of a hundred and twelve.

There are three panels rather than one, and the game picks one per position. A single tile lands its rails at the same heights in every block, so a wall repeated on a sixteen pixel rhythm however unevenly the rails were spaced inside it — unevenness cannot break a repeat that is the tile itself. What does not vary between the three is those rail heights, and it cannot: the two arms of a panel meet at its post and a panel meets the one beside it, so rails at different heights would jog up and down at every post in a run. Continuity is bought with the rails; the variation is spent on everything a seam hides.

Also

In this release

Nothing to migrate

Every change here is a texture or a model. Walls already standing keep standing, plants growing now go on growing, and a save does not know the difference.

Folders you can read

Two folders held 264 of the mod's art files between them, flat. They are nested by feature now — crop, trellis, compost, bush — and so are the Java packages the behaviour lives in.

Drawn by rule, not by hand

Every sheet in this release is derived: the dead wall from the living wall, the compost tops from Minecraft's farmland, the wall cells from the plain cucumber. A fix is a change to the rule that produced it.

The palette held still

The panel keeps its three tones and the dead sheets keep the family's four. Nothing here asked the rest of the mod's art to move with it.
